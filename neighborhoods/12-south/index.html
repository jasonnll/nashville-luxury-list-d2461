12 South Neighborhood Guide

Nashville's most photographed strip, with restored bungalows, a curated retail walk, and a relentlessly walkable lifestyle.

12 South was a forgotten strip of South Nashville until the mid-2000s. Now it is one of the most-photographed neighborhoods in the country, anchored by the I Believe in Nashville mural and a half-mile stretch of independent boutiques, restaurants, and coffee shops that brought walkability back to a city built for driving. Behind the commercial strip, blocks of restored Craftsman bungalows, Tudor cottages, and tall-skinny new-builds give 12 South a residential rhythm that is rare in Nashville.

What makes 12 South work, even now that everyone knows about it, is scale. The retail strip is only six blocks long. The residential streets behind it are narrow, shaded, and quiet. Buyers who land here typically want urbanism and walkability inside a city that does not have much of either.

Twenty years ago, the 12 South strip was largely abandoned commercial buildings and aging bungalows. The transformation has been one of the most dramatic in Nashville—but unlike East Nashville's longer, looser revival, 12 South's reinvention has been compressed into a small, walkable footprint that retains the feel of a real neighborhood rather than a destination strip.

Real Estate

The 12 South Real Estate Market

12 South remains one of Nashville's most stable luxury submarkets. As of early 2026, 85 active listings carried an average list price of about $1.36M at roughly $469 per square foot. The typical 12 South home is 2,837 square feet (3.5 bed / 3.2 bath), reflecting the prevalence of tall-and-skinny new construction alongside restored bungalows. In the September 2026 Davidson County picture, the broader city median has softened slightly (-1% YoY), but the 12 South strip has held better than the wider market thanks to its scarcity and walkability.

The $1.2M–$1.8M range has the deepest selection: new-construction townhomes and detached single-families within a five-minute walk of the strip. Above $1.8M you move into the premier new builds on the best blocks. Restored bungalows on the southern blocks closer to Sevier Park typically command a premium. For a lower entry, newer small-format condominiums near the strip start in the low $300s. Note on horizontal property regimes: two homes on one original lot are common here — review HOA documents and shared-wall details before writing an offer.

Dining

Dining in 12 South

The 12 South dining strip is dense, walkable, and equal parts neighborhood spots and destination restaurants.

  • Fine DiningLocustNo-menu prix fixe at the north end of the strip; reservations required well in advance—one of Nashville's most acclaimed.
  • CoffeeFrothy MonkeyThe original location, open since 2004. Specialty coffee, breakfast, lunch, and dinner.
  • BBQEdley's Bar-B-QueSmoked meats and the iconic Tuck Special sandwich; the patio packs out on warm evenings.
  • BurgersBurger UpLocally-sourced beef, sweet potato fries, and one of the better burgers in the city.
  • BakeryFive Daughters BakeryHome of the 100-Layer Donut—the croissant-doughnut hybrid that put 12 South on national food maps.
  • TacosBartacoCoastal-style tacos and the patio that owns 12 South on a Saturday afternoon.

Outdoors

Parks & Outdoor Recreation

12 South punches above its weight in green space thanks to Sevier Park, the largest urban park on Nashville's south side.

  • SignatureSevier Park20+ acres with two playgrounds, picnic shelters, walking paths, a creek, and the newly built community center. Hosts the 12 South Farmers Market on Tuesdays.
  • Farmers Market12 South Farmers MarketTuesday afternoons May through October; one of Nashville's most popular weekly markets.
  • TrailsBelmont Boulevard Walking LoopTree-lined connector to Belmont University and Hillsboro Village; popular morning and evening run.
  • CommunitySevier Park Community CenterNewly renovated; group fitness, after-school programs, and event rentals.

Community

Lifestyle

Life here is built around the walk: morning coffee at Frothy Monkey, an afternoon in Sevier Park, and a dozen restaurants on the strip. The 12 South Farmers Market runs on Tuesdays. There is no grocery store on the strip, so a car still helps, but most errands are short.

Downtown is about 7 minutes away and BNA about 15 off-peak. Homes are zoned for Metro Nashville Public Schools, with magnet, charter and private options for middle and high school, and Belmont University sits on the eastern edge.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions About 12 South

The questions buyers and residents ask most often about life in 12 South.

Where is 12 South in Nashville?

12 South sits south of downtown Nashville along 12th Avenue South between Edgehill and Caruthers Avenue, roughly 1.5 miles south of Music Row. The neighborhood is in the 37204 zip code and bordered by Belmont University to the east and Hillsboro Village to the west.

What is the I Believe in Nashville mural?

The original I Believe in Nashville mural by artist Adrien Saporiti is painted on the side of a building on 12th Avenue South and has become one of Nashville's most-photographed landmarks. Several other murals in 12 South—including Kelsey Montague's What Lifts You wings—anchor a walking tour that draws visitors year-round.

Are 12 South homes a good investment?

The neighborhood's price-per-square-foot has consistently outperformed the broader Nashville market, and inventory turns quickly. The most resilient submarket has been restored bungalows and new-construction homes within four blocks of the 12 South retail strip.
