Franklin Neighborhood Guide

Historic Main Street, Civil War landmarks, and one of the most-awarded small downtowns in America.

Franklin, Tennessee is the rare American small town that grew without losing itself. Just south of Brentwood, the Williamson County seat has expanded into one of the fastest-growing cities in the South while preserving the Victorian-era Main Street that earned it a Great American Main Street designation. The result is a city of roughly 90,000 that still functions like a town—where the courthouse square is the social center, the festivals are genuinely beloved, and the surrounding farmland is being carefully converted into some of the most desirable luxury developments in the region.

Franklin's real estate market reflects that scale. There are central historic neighborhoods, master-planned communities like Westhaven, gated estate communities like Laurelbrooke and Legends Ridge, golf-community homes, and working horse farms—each with its own price point and feel.

Franklin's growth has been substantial—the city has roughly doubled in population since 2000—but the historic downtown has been protected by a combination of preservation zoning, a strong Heritage Foundation, and design review. The result is a Main Street that still feels like 1890 even as the surrounding city has filled in with master-planned communities, corporate campuses, and the largest luxury inventory in the region.

Real Estate

The Franklin Real Estate Market

Franklin is several markets in one. The three-month-to-September 2026 citywide median landed at $863K, up 13% year-over-year, at roughly $321 per square foot. Of the areas tracked here, Franklin had the tightest market this quarter—homes closed in a median 55 days at 98.1% of list, better than Brentwood, Green Hills, Belle Meade, or Nashville overall. Within that citywide number, prices vary sharply by neighborhood: Central Franklin runs around $1.04M, historic downtown cottages near $550K, and master-planned areas and acreage estates significantly higher.

The luxury action sits in the master-planned communities and acreage estates on the edges. Westhaven, the traditional neighborhood development just west of downtown, runs from the high $700s to $4M+. Estate horse properties west and south of town, particularly along Old Hillsboro Road and into Leiper's Fork, regularly trade above $5M. September 2026's top regional sale was a $6.72M home on Old Hillsboro Road.

Community

Demographics & Lifestyle

Franklin's median household income ranks among the highest in Tennessee, with a population that skews family-aged and a growing concentration of corporate executives following Nissan North America, Mars Petcare, and Community Health Systems—all headquartered in or near the city. Faith plays a more central role in daily life than in Nashville proper, and the city's cultural identity is unapologetically Southern, historic, and family-oriented.

Daily life follows the rhythm of the seasons. Spring brings the Main Street Festival. Summer is concerts at the Park at Harlinsdale. Fall is Pumpkinfest, the Pilgrimage Music Festival in Harlinsdale, and the start of high school football. Winter is Dickens of a Christmas, where Main Street fills with costumed characters from the Dickens canon. The commute to downtown Nashville averages 30-35 minutes; for many residents, that is by design.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions About Franklin

The questions buyers and residents ask most often about life in Franklin.

Is Franklin TN a good place to live?

Franklin consistently ranks among the best small cities in America, with one of the highest median household incomes in Tennessee, top-rated public schools through both Williamson County Schools and the Franklin Special School District, a nationally-recognized historic downtown, and strong corporate employment from Nissan North America, Mars Petcare, and others.

What are Franklin's luxury communities?

Westhaven is the walkable master-planned community west of downtown, with pricing from the high $700s to $4M+ and a town center and golf club. Laurelbrooke, with staffed gates, and gated Legends Ridge are estate communities in north and northwest Franklin. Historic downtown offers restored Victorians, and Leiper's Fork to the west has horse farms and country estates. Just south, College Grove's The Grove and Troubadour are gated golf communities.

How far is Franklin from Nashville?

Downtown Franklin sits about 20 miles south of downtown Nashville via I-65, with typical drive times of 30-35 minutes outside rush hour. The BNA airport is about 30-35 minutes northeast.
