East Nashville Neighborhood Guide

Five Points, Lockeland Springs, and Inglewood—the city's creative heart, with the food scene to prove it.

East Nashville is less a single neighborhood than a collection of distinct ones: Five Points, Edgefield, Lockeland Springs, Eastwood, Cleveland Park, and Inglewood, each with its own architectural character and price band. What they share is geography—everything east of the Cumberland River, north of Shelby Park—and a creative culture built by musicians, chefs, and designers who arrived in the early 2000s and never left.

The result is the most stylistically distinctive part of Nashville. Restored Victorians sit beside modern infill. Coffee shops outnumber chains. The restaurant scene is consistently the city's most exciting. For buyers, East Nashville offers genuine architectural diversity at a price point that—while no longer cheap—remains below the West Nashville prestige zones.

Buyers should understand that East Nashville is a collection of sub-neighborhoods rather than a single market. Edgefield and Lockeland Springs sit on the highest end. Cleveland Park and Eastwood are mid-market. Inglewood north of Briley Parkway offers the deepest value and the most appreciation upside. Five Points sits at the geographic and cultural center.

Real Estate

The East Nashville Real Estate Market

East Nashville's market has normalized from its 2022 peak while the broader Davidson County median sits roughly flat year-over-year. In the 37206 zip code, the median sale price was about $740K for the three months ending August 2026, up about 3% from a year earlier, with homes selling in a median of 56 days at 98.3% of list. The pullback from peak pace reflects normalization after years of double-digit appreciation, not weakness, and inventory has grown enough to give buyers more negotiating room than at any point in the last five years.

The luxury tier is concentrated in Lockeland Springs and Edgefield, where restored Victorians and Craftsman bungalows regularly close between $900K and $1.6M. Architect-designed new construction above $1.5M clusters on the higher streets of Eastwood and East End. Inglewood, north of Briley Parkway, offers the deepest value and is the sub-neighborhood most often discussed as East Nashville's next appreciation story. Five Points-adjacent homes typically run $500K–$900K and move fastest.

Dining

Dining in East Nashville

East Nashville has been the most dynamic restaurant neighborhood in the city for fifteen years. The list below skews toward institutions; new openings happen monthly.

  • Fine DiningMargot Cafe & BarMargot McCormack's French-Southern bistro in a converted gas station; menu changes daily based on what farms deliver.
  • PizzaFive Points PizzaHand-tossed New York slices and one of the better pizza patios in the city.
  • TacosMas Tacos Por FavorCult-favorite taqueria; fried chicken tacos, pozole, and one of the city's great street menus.
  • ItalianLockeland TableWood-fired pizzas, Sunday suppers, and a beloved community-funded model that supports East Nashville schools.
  • BrunchMarché Artisan FoodsEuropean-inspired café with one of the best weekend brunches in the neighborhood.
  • BarThe Pharmacy Burger ParlorGerman biergarten, classic burgers, and the patio that defines an East Nashville Saturday.

Outdoors

Parks & Outdoor Recreation

East Nashville is the greenest part of the city east of the river, anchored by one of Nashville's largest parks.

  • SignatureShelby Park & Bottoms1,200+ acres along the Cumberland; trails, lakes, golf course, sports fields, and the Shelby Bottoms Greenway.
  • GreenwayShelby Bottoms Greenway5+ miles of paved paths connecting East Nashville to Two Rivers Park via the Cumberland River pedestrian bridge.
  • LocalEast ParkEdgefield's neighborhood green space; playground, community garden, and the East Nashville Farmers Market.
  • GolfShelby Park Golf CoursePublic course inside Shelby Park; one of Nashville's most affordable public rounds.

Education

Schools & Education

East Nashville's schools have improved meaningfully over the past decade, with several strong magnet and charter options available across the zone.

Public ElementaryLockeland Design Center

Highly-regarded Metro Schools elementary in Lockeland Springs; design-integrated curriculum.

Public MagnetMeigs Magnet Middle School

Competitive admission magnet on the East Nashville side of the river; one of Metro's top middle schools.

Community

Lifestyle

Daily life runs at bike-and-coffee-shop scale: Barista Parlor or Ugly Mugs in the morning, groceries at the Turnip Truck, and dinner around Five Points. Shelby Park and the Shelby Bottoms Greenway sit on the east edge.

Downtown is about a 5-minute drive, with the East Bank development next door, yet the streets keep a residential, neighborhood feel. Schools include several strong magnet and charter options across the zone, including the Stratford STEM magnet high school.

Things to Do

Nearby Attractions

East Nashville's geography puts the entire city within easy reach—and the neighborhood has plenty of its own.

  • MusicThe Basement EastBeloved live music venue; intimate shows from touring and Nashville artists.
  • ShoppingRiverside Village & Five PointsTwo walkable retail clusters with indie boutiques, coffee, and dining.
  • DevelopmentThe East BankThe riverfront district just south and west, home to Nissan Stadium and one of the city's largest redevelopment plans.
  • GreenwayCumberland River GreenwayPedestrian connections across the river into downtown, including the John Seigenthaler Pedestrian Bridge.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions About East Nashville

The questions buyers and residents ask most often about life in East Nashville.

What is Five Points in East Nashville?

Five Points is the central intersection of East Nashville, where Woodland Street and several neighborhood streets converge. It serves as the neighborhood's commercial and cultural anchor, with restaurants like Margot Cafe, Five Points Pizza, and Mas Tacos all within a short walk.

Is East Nashville safe?

East Nashville has changed dramatically over the past 15 years and is considered safe in most areas, particularly the core sub-neighborhoods like Lockeland Springs, Edgefield, Eastwood, and the streets within a few blocks of Five Points. Buyers should research individual blocks since conditions can vary street to street.

What schools serve East Nashville?

East Nashville is served by Metro Nashville Public Schools, with strong options including Lockeland Design Center (elementary), Meigs Magnet Middle School, and Stratford STEM High School. Public charter networks including KIPP Nashville and East End Prep also operate campuses in the area.
