Green Hills Neighborhood Guide

Walkable luxury, Nashville's best shopping, and the city's most coveted school zone.

Green Hills is the Nashville neighborhood that does almost everything well. It's walkable enough to feel urban, leafy enough to feel residential, and central enough that residents joke they can be anywhere in town in fifteen minutes. The Mall at Green Hills anchors the southern end—the highest-grossing shopping mall in Tennessee—while quiet streets of brick traditionals and modern new-builds spread north and east into some of the city's most coveted school zones.

Among Nashville's prestige neighborhoods, Green Hills sits in a sweet spot: more accessible than Belle Meade, more established than 12 South, quieter than East Nashville. Buyers come for the 37215 zip code and stay for the convenience.

Compared to Belle Meade, Green Hills is more architecturally varied and more accessible. Compared to 12 South, it is more established and more spread out. Compared to Brentwood, it offers a shorter commute and a denser, more walkable feel. For many Nashville buyers, the 37215 zip code is the single most-searched filter on a relocation hunt—and Green Hills sits at the heart of it.

Real Estate

The Green Hills Real Estate Market

Green Hills has been one of the strongest-performing Davidson County submarkets this year. The three-month-to-June 2026 median landed at $1.25M (up 14% year-over-year), with price per square foot at $439 (up 22% YoY) and homes closing at 96.9% of list on a median 62 days on market. The range is wider than buyers expect: a renovated 1950s brick ranch on a 0.25-acre lot might list in the $900Ks, while a new-construction transitional on Estes Road or Lynnwood Boulevard regularly clears $3M.

The most competitive segment is single-family homes between $1.2M and $1.9M, where demand is strongest, much of it driven by the school zoning. Above $2.5M, the market is dominated by tear-down-and-rebuilds where the lot value justifies a custom project. Full-service condominium buildings near Hillsboro Road offer a lock-and-leave entry starting in the high $400s and appeal to buyers who want a Nashville base without a yard.

Dining

Dining in Green Hills

Green Hills offers Nashville's most consistent collection of established restaurants. The mix runs from white-tablecloth steakhouses to neighborhood cafés, with The Mall at Green Hills serving as a second dining hub.

  • Fine DiningF. Scott's Restaurant & Jazz BarLive jazz nightly, an inventive American menu, and one of the most polished wine programs in the city.
  • SteakhouseChar RestaurantLive piano, classic steakhouse menu, and the kind of crowd that books the same table every Friday.
  • SouthernFirefly GrilleWood-fired Southern cooking; the patio is one of Nashville's best for a long lunch.
  • ItalianSole MioLong-running family Italian; the kind of place where the owner remembers your kids' names.
  • CasualPancake PantryHillsboro Village institution. Yes, the line is real. Yes, it's worth it.
  • MusicBluebird CafeThe 90-seat songwriter listening room that launched Garth Brooks and Taylor Swift—technically Green Hills.

Outdoors

Parks & Outdoor Recreation

Green Hills is more residential than recreational, but the surrounding areas offer some of the city's best parks within a five-minute drive.

  • LocalSunnyside ParkNeighborhood green space with playground, tennis courts, and the Sevier Park-style energy on weekends.
  • HikingRadnor Lake State Park1,332-acre Class II Natural Area five minutes south. 6+ miles of trails, no jogging or bikes—a quiet retreat.
  • FamilyCrockett Park (Brentwood)165 acres just south on Franklin Road; ball fields, playground, and the Eddy Arnold Amphitheater.
  • Country ClubHillwood Country ClubMembers-only golf and tennis just over the western border in Belle Meade.

Community

Lifestyle

Daily life centers on Hillsboro Pike: coffee at Frothy Monkey or Crema, errands at Whole Foods or Trader Joe's, and evenings at the Bluebird Cafe, a Belmont basketball game or dinner at F. Scott's. Vanderbilt and Saint Thomas hospitals are within ten minutes.

Downtown is about 12 minutes away and the airport about 18, and the central location keeps most errands short. Green Hills is one of Nashville's strongest public school zones and home to several of its leading private schools.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions About Green Hills

The questions buyers and residents ask most often about life in Green Hills.

What zip code is Green Hills?

Green Hills sits in the 37215 zip code—one of the most desirable in Nashville and a major search filter for relocating buyers. The same zip code also covers parts of Forest Hills and Oak Hill.

Is Green Hills walkable?

More walkable than most of Nashville. The Mall at Green Hills, Hillsboro Pike retail, Bluebird Cafe, Parnassus Books, Whole Foods, and Trader Joe's are all within a short walk or bike ride of most homes in the neighborhood's core, with Walk Scores in the 60s for the central blocks.

What schools serve Green Hills?

Green Hills is zoned for Julia Green Elementary, John Trotwood Moore Middle School, and Hillsboro High School (which offers the International Baccalaureate program)—one of Metro Nashville's strongest public school zones. Private options include Lipscomb Academy and Christ Presbyterian Academy.
