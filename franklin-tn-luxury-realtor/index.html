Hiring an agent

How to choose the best luxury realtor in Franklin

Franklin's luxury homes range from pre-1900 Victorians off Main Street to new builds in Westhaven and 50-acre farms past Leiper's Fork. Each needs different expertise. Ask any agent these questions first.

01

Do they know which school district a home is in?

Central Franklin is served by Franklin Special School District for kindergarten through eighth grade, with Williamson County Schools for high school; the rest of the city is Williamson County Schools throughout. The district can change from one street to the next.

02

Can they handle historic homes?

Homes in and around downtown Franklin can fall under historic zoning, which means exterior changes and additions need city review. Your agent should check the overlay before you plan a renovation.

03

Do they know acreage due diligence?

Estate and farm properties toward Leiper's Fork often run on well and septic, with easements, fencing and outbuildings to inspect. Those checks belong in the offer timeline.

04

Can they negotiate with builders?

Much of Franklin's luxury inventory is new or recent construction in communities such as Westhaven and Ladd Park. Builder contracts, upgrades and warranty terms are negotiable if your agent knows where to push.

05

Are they ready to move fast?

Franklin homes sold in a median of 49 days and about 17% sold above list. Buyers need pre-approval or proof of funds and an agent who can show the same day.

06

Will they price a listing honestly?

Franklin's citywide median is $863,000, so a $2M+ listing is competing in a much thinner pool. Ask for the comparable sales at your price, not the city average.

Franklin luxury real estate FAQ

Questions buyers and sellers ask first

Who is a good luxury realtor in Franklin, TN?
Look for an agent who works the $1.5M+ tier in Williamson County, knows the Franklin Special School District boundaries and historic overlays, and can show recent closings. Jason Kloess of Compass covers Franklin's luxury market alongside nearby Thompson's Station, where he closed 2939 Ave Downs Drive at $1.56 million, and he publishes the weekly Nashville Luxury List of $1.5M+ homes.
What does a luxury home cost in Franklin, TN?
Franklin's citywide median was $862,929 for the three months ending August 2026, up 12.4% year over year. Luxury homes typically run from about $1.5 million to $7 million and up; 3029 William Street sold for about $2.55 million in October 2026, and September's top sale was $6.72 million on Old Hillsboro Road.
What are the most exclusive neighborhoods in Franklin?
Laurelbrooke, with staffed gates on nearly 600 wooded acres, and Legends Ridge in northwest Franklin lead for estates. Westhaven is the most active luxury community, historic downtown offers walkable Victorians, and Leiper's Fork to the west has horse farms and country estates on large acreage.
Which school district serves Franklin homes?
It depends on the address. Central Franklin is served by Franklin Special School District for grades K–8, and Williamson County Schools serves high school and the rest of the city. Moore, Franklin and Oak View elementary schools each hold a 10/10 GreatSchools rating. Confirm the zoning for each property with the district.
How much is property tax on a luxury home in Franklin?
Tennessee assesses homes at 25% of appraised value. On a $3 million home, Franklin's 2026 rate of $1.62 works out to about $12,150 a year, plus the FSSD levy where a home is zoned for it. That is a little over half of Nashville's roughly $21,105.
How fast are Franklin homes selling?
Homes sold in a median of 49 days at 98.3% of list price for the three months ending August 2026, and about 17% sold above list, up nearly five points from a year earlier.
How far is Franklin from downtown Nashville?
About 20 miles, typically 25 to 35 minutes by car via I-65 outside rush hour. Brentwood sits between the two and is covered on the Brentwood page.
How much does a luxury realtor cost?
Commission is negotiable and is set in writing. Sellers agree to it in the listing agreement. Since the 2024 NAR settlement, buyers also sign a written agreement with their agent before touring that states the agent's compensation; it is often paid by the seller as a negotiated term, but that is not guaranteed.
