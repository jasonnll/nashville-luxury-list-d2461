Belle Meade vs. Green Hills

Belle Meade costs far more per square foot because you are buying land and permanence: it is a separate city, incorporated in 1938, with its own police and zoning, estate lots of one to two acres, and a median sale price of $4.30 million at about $880 a square foot. Green Hills is part of Metro Nashville, more walkable and more varied, with lots around a third of an acre and a median of $1.25 million at about $439 a square foot. The same budget buys a much larger house in Green Hills; it buys a larger lot and a quieter street in Belle Meade.

  • Belle Meade: about twice the price per square foot of Green Hills
  • Property tax on a $3M home is close: about $20,865 in Belle Meade (plus the city levy) vs $21,105 in Green Hills
  • Both are Metro Nashville Public Schools; private schools are a short drive from either
  • Green Hills is the better fit for walkability and new construction; Belle Meade for land and privacy

The difference

What you are actually paying for

Belle Meade trades on land more than square footage. The neighborhood guide puts it simply: a $3 million home in Belle Meade may be the same size as a $1.4 million home in Green Hills. The difference is the lot, the trees, the address and zoning that keeps the street the way it is. Belle Meade residents voted to incorporate in 1938 specifically to control zoning, and the city still runs its own police department and design review.

Green Hills is the more practical choice for most buyers. It sits around the Mall at Green Hills and Hillsboro Pike, so groceries, restaurants and Radnor Lake are close, and Vanderbilt and Midtown are about 8 to 10 minutes away. The housing stock runs from renovated 1950s brick ranches in the $900Ks to new construction on streets such as Estes Road and Lynnwood Boulevard that regularly clears $3 million.

Choosing

Which one fits you

Choose Belle Meade if the lot matters more than the house: an acre or two, mature trees, little through-traffic, and a market where the best homes on Belle Meade Boulevard or backing to Percy Warner Park rarely list publicly. Expect fewer sales (13 closings in the quarter behind the median above), so each sale moves the numbers.

Choose Green Hills if you want more house for the money, newer construction and the ability to run errands without a long drive. The most competitive segment is $1.2 million to $1.9 million, where school zoning drives demand; above about $2.5 million, most of the market is tear-down-and-rebuild on a good lot.

Taxes are not the deciding factor here: both are in Davidson County at nearly the same rate, and Belle Meade adds a small city levy. The real cost difference is the purchase price per square foot.

FAQ

Questions, answered

Is Belle Meade more expensive than Green Hills?
Yes, by about double per square foot. Belle Meade's median sale price was $4,297,155 ($880 a square foot) over the three months to August 2026; Green Hills' was $1,249,565 ($439 a square foot) over the three months to June 2026 (Redfin).
Is Belle Meade a separate city from Nashville?
Yes. Belle Meade incorporated in 1938 and has its own police department and zoning, inside Davidson County. Green Hills is a neighborhood of Metro Nashville.
What is the property tax on a $3 million home in Belle Meade vs Green Hills?
About $20,865 a year in Belle Meade at the Davidson County General Services rate of $2.782 per $100 of assessed value, plus Belle Meade's city levy, and about $21,105 in Green Hills at the Urban Services rate of $2.814. Tennessee assesses homes at 25% of appraised value.
Which schools serve Belle Meade and Green Hills?
Both are zoned for Metro Nashville Public Schools. Much of Green Hills is zoned for Julia Green or Percy Priest Elementary, John Trotwood Moore Middle and Hillsboro High. Zoning depends on the street, so confirm any address with MNPS.
What ZIP codes are Belle Meade and Green Hills?
Belle Meade is mostly 37205. Green Hills is 37215, which also covers parts of Forest Hills and Oak Hill.
