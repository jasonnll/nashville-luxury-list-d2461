Davidson County vs. Williamson County

On a luxury home, the county line mostly changes two things: property tax and public schools. Williamson County (Brentwood, Franklin, College Grove) runs roughly half the property tax of Davidson County: about $11,175 a year on a $3 million home in Brentwood against about $21,105 in Green Hills. Williamson is served by Williamson County Schools (and the Franklin Special School District in parts of Franklin); Davidson by Metro Nashville Public Schools, with most of the city's private schools. Davidson has the in-town estate areas, from Belle Meade to Green Hills, 12 to 15 minutes from downtown.

  • Property tax on a $3M home: about $21,105 in Nashville's Urban Services District vs $11,175 in Brentwood
  • Over ten years that gap is close to $100,000
  • Williamson County's median listing price was $1,155,000 in September 2026; Davidson's was $533,117 in August
  • Some neighborhoods straddle the line (part of Annandale is in Davidson), so check by parcel

The trade-off

What you get on each side

Davidson County has Nashville's established in-town estate areas: Belle Meade, Forest Hills and Oak Hill (General Services District, typically one to three acres), and Green Hills, 12 South, Sylvan Park and East Nashville (Urban Services District, smaller lots, more walkable). Downtown, Vanderbilt and the airport are 5 to 20 minutes away, and most of the region's independent private schools are here.

Williamson County is where most families buying a $1.5 million-plus home for the public schools look: Brentwood's one-acre neighborhoods and gated communities, Franklin's town center, master-planned communities and estates, and the club communities and farms of College Grove and Leiper's Fork. Drives into Nashville run 20 to 40 minutes.

Lower tax is part of why Williamson homes carry a premium: over a decade, the difference on a $3 million home is close to six figures.

FAQ

Questions, answered

Is property tax lower in Williamson County than in Davidson County?
Yes, roughly half. At 2026 rates, a $3 million home costs about $11,175 a year in Brentwood, $12,150 in Franklin and $9,750 in unincorporated Williamson County, against about $21,105 in Nashville's Urban Services District and $20,865 in the General Services District (plus any city levy, such as Belle Meade's).
How is property tax calculated in Tennessee?
Homes are assessed at 25% of appraised value, and the local rate is applied per $100 of assessed value. A $3 million home is assessed at $750,000; at Brentwood's $1.49 rate the bill is $11,175.
Does Tennessee have a state income tax?
No. Tennessee does not tax wages or investment income, in either county.
Which Nashville-area neighborhoods are in Williamson County?
Brentwood, Franklin, Thompson's Station, Nolensville, Spring Hill, College Grove, Arrington and Leiper's Fork. Belle Meade, Forest Hills, Oak Hill, Green Hills, 12 South and East Nashville are in Davidson County.
Which public schools serve each county?
Davidson County is Metro Nashville Public Schools. Williamson County is Williamson County Schools, plus the Franklin Special School District for kindergarten through eighth grade in parts of Franklin.
