Privacy and ownership checklistWhat becomes public, and how buyers keep their name off it
+
  • Deeds are public. In Tennessee, the buyer named on the deed and the sale price can be looked up through county property records.
  • Decide title early. Trusts and LLCs are common for high-profile buyers. Set it up with the client’s attorney and CPA before the contract, since the buyer named on the contract matters.
  • Financing affects structure. Some lenders won’t lend to an LLC. Talk to the lender and attorney together if there will be a mortgage.
  • Mind the paper trail. Utilities, HOA records, permits and insurance can carry a name too. Plan who signs what.
  • Use code names. In email, texts, showing requests and the CRM, until closing and after.

General information, not legal or tax advice. The client’s attorney and CPA should set the structure.

What artists ask for in a homeThe checklist Jason uses before a showing
+
  • Privacy: gate, long drive, setback from the road, sight lines from neighbors and public roads.
  • Studio: room to isolate sound, electrical capacity, HVAC noise, and whether commercial sessions fit local home-occupation rules.
  • Vehicles: parking and turning room for a tour bus, trailer or sprinter, and a road that can take one.
  • Guests and staff: guest house, apartment or separate quarters for crew, family or security.
  • Gear: climate-controlled storage for instruments and equipment.
  • Logistics: drive to BNA, Music Row and the studios they use most.
  • Security: existing cameras, gate controls and who already has codes.

Tennessee money notesCosts a business manager will want on the sheet
+
  • No state income tax on wages. One reason so many artists base themselves here. Ask the CPA how it applies to touring and royalty income.
  • Transfer tax: $0.37 per $100 of the sale price, recorded with the deed. Who pays is negotiable.
  • Mortgage tax: $0.115 per $100 of the loan amount above $2,000, if financing.
  • Property tax differs by county and city. See Davidson vs Williamson County.
  • Carrying costs: high-value home insurance, HOA or club dues, security and staff belong in the budget next to the mortgage.

Rates per the Tennessee Department of Revenue, November 2026. Not tax advice.

FAQ

Questions artists and their teams ask

Can a home purchase in Nashville be kept private?
Deeds and sale prices are public record in Tennessee, so the buyer named on the deed can be looked up. Many high-profile buyers take title in a trust or LLC set up with their attorney before the contract is signed, so the client's personal name does not appear. Jason also uses code names in emails, showing requests and his CRM.
Why work with an agent from the music industry?
Jason spent more than a decade as a national touring artist, writing, recording, managing and touring. He understands album and tour cycles, how managers, business managers and attorneys work together, and what an artist needs from a home, from studio space to bus parking to privacy.
Will you sign an NDA?
Yes. Jason will sign your attorney's NDA, or provide one, before learning the client's identity or scheduling the first showing.
Can my client buy without seeing every home in person?
Yes. Jason previews homes first and sends private video walk-throughs, so the client only tours the short list. Closings can be signed remotely, which works around tour schedules.
Can an artist sell a home without it being public?
Yes. Through a Compass Private Exclusive, a home can be offered quietly to Compass agents and vetted buyers without appearing on public sites, and without the owner's name in the marketing.
Who should I talk to if I'm the manager or business manager?
Talk to Jason directly. He works through whoever the client designates, keeps the business manager on budget, proof of funds and closing numbers, and keeps the attorney on contract and ownership structure.
