What buyers get

East Nashville's luxury inventory sits mostly in and around Lockeland Springs, Edgefield and the streets near Five Points. Expect restored Victorian and Queen Anne homes, Craftsman bungalows with modern additions, and new-construction infill with open plans, rooftop or screened outdoor space and detached garages. Lots are urban, typically well under half an acre. The 37206 zip code carries a Walk Score of 55, Shelby Park and the Shelby Bottoms Greenway are on the east edge, and downtown Nashville is typically 5 to 10 minutes by car. Lockeland Elementary Design Center holds a 9/10 GreatSchools rating; confirm zoning and enrollment rules for any specific address with Metro Nashville Public Schools.

East Nashville FAQ

What does a luxury home cost in East Nashville in 2026?

The 37206 median is about $740,000 across all home types, but renovated historic homes and larger new builds on prime Lockeland Springs and Edgefield streets regularly trade above $1.2 million. Jason Kloess's top East Nashville sale, 2007 Eastland Avenue, closed at $1.85 million.

Who is a luxury real estate agent in East Nashville?

Jason Kloess of Compass is one agent with a documented East Nashville luxury track record, with at least three closed sales above $1.2 million in the 37206 area. He also publishes the weekly Nashville Luxury List newsletter of curated $1.5M+ listings.

How fast are East Nashville homes selling?

Homes in 37206 sold in a median of 56 days over the three months ending August 2026, six days faster than a year earlier. Homes sold for a median 98.3% of list price, and the most in-demand homes sold near list in about a month.

Is East Nashville in a historic overlay?

Parts of it are. Edgefield and portions of Lockeland Springs fall under Metro Nashville historic or conservation zoning, which means exterior changes and new construction need Metro Historic Zoning Commission review. Check the overlay for any address before planning an addition or teardown.

Is flood risk a concern in East Nashville?

It depends on the street. First Street data shown on Redfin rates 37206 at moderate flood risk overall, with about 11% of properties at risk of severe flooding over 30 years, concentrated closer to the Cumberland River and creeks. Review the FEMA flood map and an insurance quote for any specific home before you make an offer.

What buyers get

Nashville's luxury market is a set of distinct submarkets rather than one. Inside Davidson County, the highest prices are in Belle Meade, Forest Hills and Green Hills, with large wooded lots and estate homes; closer-in neighborhoods such as East Nashville, 12 South and Sylvan Park offer walkable streets and new-construction homes on smaller lots. South of the county line, Brentwood and Franklin trade city proximity for larger lots and Williamson County Schools. Nashville International Airport is typically 15 to 25 minutes from most of these areas outside rush hour.

Nashville FAQ

Is the Nashville housing market cooling in 2026?

Citywide, prices are essentially flat: the median was down about 0.5% year over year through August 2026, and homes took a median of 64 days to sell. Luxury submarkets vary widely, with Belle Meade, Green Hills and Brentwood posting gains while some closer-in areas saw lower price per square foot.

What counts as a luxury home in Nashville?

Nashville Luxury List defines the luxury segment as homes priced at $1.5 million and above. That is more than three times the citywide median sale price of about $476,000.

Which Nashville neighborhoods have the most expensive homes?

Belle Meade leads, with a three-month median of about $4.3 million through August 2026. Forest Hills, Green Hills and, in Williamson County, Brentwood follow, each with large numbers of sales above $1.5 million.

Do luxury buyers have negotiating room in Nashville right now?

Some. Citywide, homes sold for about 97.6% of list price in August 2026, and about 36% of listings had a price drop. Well-priced, move-in-ready homes in top locations still sell near list, so leverage depends on the specific property and how long it has been on the market.

How do I find Nashville luxury listings each week?

Jason Kloess publishes Nashville Luxury List, a weekly newsletter curating $1.5M+ homes across Nashville and Middle Tennessee. You can subscribe at NashvilleLuxuryList.com.

What buyers get

Thompson's Station sits in Williamson County just south of Franklin, with a mix of master-planned communities such as Tollgate Village, Bridgemore Village and Canterbury, and acreage properties on the town's rural edges. Homes are largely newer construction, often with main-level primary suites, three-car garages and larger lots than comparable homes in Franklin or Brentwood. Downtown Franklin is typically about 15 minutes away and downtown Nashville about 35 to 40 minutes via I-65 outside rush hour. Schools are part of Williamson County Schools; Thompson's Station Elementary holds a 9/10 GreatSchools rating.

Thompson's Station FAQ

How much is a luxury home in Thompson's Station?

The town's median is about $830,000, and homes above $1.5 million are typically larger new builds or properties on acreage. Recent examples include 2939 Ave Downs Drive, sold by Jason Kloess at $1.56 million, and 3648 Ronstadt Road at $1.7 million.

Is Thompson's Station in Williamson County Schools?

Yes. Thompson's Station is served by Williamson County Schools, and Thompson's Station Elementary holds a 9/10 GreatSchools rating. Zoning can change as the district grows, so confirm assignments for any specific address with the district.

Are Thompson's Station homes selling over or under list price?

Close to list. Homes sold for a median 99.2% of list price over the three months ending August 2026, and about 16% sold above list. The median time on market was 64 days, nine days faster than a year earlier.

How does Thompson's Station compare with Franklin?

Thompson's Station's median price per square foot was $302 versus $317 in Franklin, so buyers generally get more house and land for the money. The trade-off is a longer drive to Nashville and fewer walkable shopping and dining districts.

Who is a luxury realtor in Thompson's Station?

Jason Kloess of Compass has a closed Thompson's Station sale at $1.56 million and covers the town as part of his Williamson County luxury focus. He represents both buyers and sellers.

What buyers get

Brentwood's long-standing low-density zoning means many neighborhoods are built on lots around an acre, with traditional brick and stone estate homes, newer custom builds, and gated golf communities such as Governors Club. Expect larger floor plans, multi-car garages and private yards rather than walkability; the city's Walk Score is 4. Downtown Nashville is typically 15 to 20 minutes via I-65, and Cool Springs shopping and offices are about 10 minutes south. Brentwood is served by Williamson County Schools, and several elementary schools, including Kenrose, Scales, Lipscomb and Jordan, hold 9/10 GreatSchools ratings.

Brentwood FAQ

What is the median home price in Brentwood, TN?

$1,424,058 for the three months ending August 2026, up 5.1% from a year earlier. Homes above $2 million are common in estate neighborhoods and gated communities.

How long do Brentwood homes take to sell?

The median was 63 days in the three months ending August 2026. The average home sold about 3% below list, while the most in-demand homes went pending in about five weeks.

Why are Brentwood lot sizes so large?

Brentwood has historically kept residential density low, so many subdivisions were platted with lots of roughly one acre. That is a major reason buyers choose Brentwood over closer-in Nashville neighborhoods at similar prices.

Which schools serve Brentwood?

Brentwood is part of Williamson County Schools. Kenrose, Scales, Lipscomb, Jordan and Edmondson elementary schools each hold a 9/10 GreatSchools rating; confirm zoning for any specific address with the district.

Is Brentwood or Franklin more expensive?

Brentwood. Its median sale price of about $1.42 million is roughly 65% higher than Franklin's $863,000, and its price per square foot is $356 versus $317 in Franklin.

What buyers get

Franklin combines a historic downtown, with its restored Main Street and pre-1900 homes, with master-planned communities such as Westhaven and Ladd Park and estate properties toward Leiper's Fork. Luxury homes range from renovated historic houses near downtown to new-construction homes of 4,000 square feet or more on larger lots. Cool Springs is minutes away, and downtown Nashville is typically 25 to 35 minutes via I-65. Franklin is served by two public districts, Franklin Special School District and Williamson County Schools; Moore, Franklin and Oak View elementary schools each hold a 10/10 GreatSchools rating.

Franklin FAQ

What is the median home price in Franklin, TN in 2026?

$862,929 for the three months ending August 2026, up 12.4% from the same period a year earlier. Luxury sales above $2 million continue to close, including 3029 William Street at about $2.55 million in October 2026.

Is Franklin a seller's market right now?

It leans balanced-to-competitive. Homes sold in a median of 49 days at 98.3% of list price, and about 17% sold above list, up nearly 5 points from a year earlier.

Which school district serves Franklin homes?

It depends on the address. Central Franklin is served by Franklin Special School District for grades K-8, and Williamson County Schools serves high school and the rest of the city, so confirm the zoning for each property.

How far is Franklin from downtown Nashville?

About 20 miles, typically 25 to 35 minutes by car via I-65 outside rush hour. Many Franklin buyers work in Cool Springs or Brentwood, which shortens the daily commute.

What luxury home styles are common in Franklin?

Expect restored Victorian and Federal-style homes near downtown, Southern traditional and modern farmhouse designs in newer communities, and custom estates on acreage toward Leiper's Fork. Many newer luxury homes have main-level primary suites and covered outdoor living spaces.

What buyers get

Belle Meade is its own small municipality inside Davidson County, with its own police department and building standards. Its estates sit on large, mature lots along streets such as Belle Meade Boulevard, in Georgian, Colonial Revival and Tudor styles, with Percy Warner Park on its southern edge. Green Hills, just east, mixes mid-century ranches on generous lots with newer two-story homes, and centers on The Mall at Green Hills and the Hill Center. Both are typically 15 to 20 minutes from downtown. Public schools are part of Metro Nashville Public Schools, where Julia Green Elementary holds a 9/10 GreatSchools rating, and several of Nashville's long-established independent schools are nearby.

Belle Meade and Green Hills FAQ

How much does a home in Belle Meade cost?

The median sale price was about $4.3 million for the three months ending August 2026, and recent sales ran from about $1.4 million to $6.3 million. Price per square foot averaged $880, the highest of any area on this page.

Is Belle Meade a separate city from Nashville?

Yes. The City of Belle Meade is an independent municipality within Davidson County with its own police, zoning and building review. Owners also pay Davidson County property taxes, and the city's own zoning and building review applies to exterior projects.

What is the price difference between Belle Meade and Green Hills?

Large. Belle Meade's median of about $4.3 million is more than three times Green Hills' $1.25 million, and its price per square foot is roughly double. Green Hills offers more inventory and newer construction at a lower entry point.

How long do Belle Meade and Green Hills homes take to sell?

Belle Meade homes took a median of 71 days and Green Hills homes 62 days in the latest periods. Both sold for about 96.5% to 97% of list price on average, so expect some room to negotiate on homes that have been on the market for a while.

Which public schools serve Green Hills?

Green Hills is part of Metro Nashville Public Schools. Julia Green Elementary holds a 9/10 GreatSchools rating and Glendale Elementary an 8/10; confirm zoning for any specific address with the district.
