Can I buy a Nashville home without living here?

Yes. Most relocating buyers do it with one scouting trip and the rest from home. Narrow the areas first (the county line changes property tax and schools), then spend a weekend touring neighborhoods rather than houses. After that your agent previews homes and runs live video tours, attends inspections and walk-throughs, and the closing can be handled electronically or by mail. Have proof of funds or a pre-approval ready before you visit so you can act if the right home comes up.

  • Choose the area before the house: Davidson vs Williamson changes taxes and schools
  • One scouting weekend; everything after can be remote
  • Your agent attends showings, inspections and the final walk-through
  • Have proof of funds or pre-approval ready before the trip

The steps

How a remote purchase works

01

Pick the area

Decide between Davidson and Williamson County first: it changes property tax by roughly half and the public schools.

02

Get ready to act

Pre-approval or proof of funds before you travel, so you can make an offer on a scouting trip.

03

One scouting weekend

Tour neighborhoods and a handful of homes to calibrate what your budget buys.

04

Search from home

Your agent previews homes, runs live video tours and sends private and off-market homes as they come up.

05

Offer and inspect

Your agent attends inspections and walk-throughs and handles repairs and credits.

06

Close

Sign electronically or by mail where the title company allows, and get your keys.

FAQ

Questions, answered

Can I close on a Tennessee home remotely?
Often, yes. Many closings are done electronically or with documents signed and notarized where you are. Ask the title company early what they accept.
Should I rent first?
If you are unsure between Davidson and Williamson County, renting for a season can help. If you know the area, buying from a distance with a local agent is common.
