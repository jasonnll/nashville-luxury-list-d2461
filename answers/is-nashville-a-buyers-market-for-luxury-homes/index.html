Is Nashville a buyer's market for luxury homes?

At $1 million and up, it leans toward buyers. At the end of September 2026 about 2,289 homes priced at $1 million or more were for sale across Middle Tennessee against roughly 460 closings a month, about five months of supply. 43% of active $1M+ listings were below their original asking price. That gives buyers room to negotiate on homes that have sat, while the best-priced homes in the best locations still sell quickly and sometimes with competition.

  • About five months of $1M+ supply in September 2026
  • 43% of active $1M+ listings had cut their price
  • Regional closings down 10% year over year; inventory up 3%
  • Williamson County's median listing price held near $1.16M

What it means

How to use it

Buyers: look at days on market and price history before you offer. A home that has been listed for 60 days or more, or has already cut its price, is where the negotiating room is. Seller credits and inspection repairs are back on the table at this price point.

Sellers: the price at launch matters more than it did in 2021 or 2022. Homes that price to the last few comparable sales are still selling; homes that price on hope are joining the 43%.

FAQ

Questions, answered

Are Nashville luxury home prices going down?
Not across the board. Some areas rose: Belle Meade's median was up 37% year over year and Green Hills' 14% in the latest quarters we track. But nearly half of $1M+ listings cut their asking price in fall 2026, so list prices are adjusting.
How much can I negotiate on a $2 million Nashville home?
It depends on the home's days on market and price history. Ask your agent for the sale-to-list ratio of the last few comparable sales; in the areas we track it ran from about 96.5% to 98.3% of list price.
